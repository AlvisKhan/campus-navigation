Reading light

How can light tell us whether one molecule has slipped inside another? Explore what happens when a fluorescent guest meets a tiny molecular host.

01 · The molecules

A small guest and a hollow host

The guest gives off light. The host gives the guest a different place to sit. If that new environment changes the light we detect, it gives us a clue that the molecules are interacting.

Guest

Salicylic acid

A benzene ring with two neighbouring groups, hydroxyl (–OH) and carboxylic acid (–COOH). It is also called 2-hydroxybenzoic acid. You may recognise it from skincare.

It can absorb UV light and fluoresce on its own. At pH 7, its carboxyl group is mostly deprotonated (–COO−), giving salicylate.

Host

β-Cyclodextrin

Seven glucose units form a ring shaped like a short, hollow cup. The outer surface interacts well with water. The cavity is relatively less polar than the water around it.

A suitably sized guest may fit partly inside. The molecules can associate and separate in solution.

02 · The physics

What goes in, and what comes out?

A spectrum is a graph of a light signal against wavelength. The horizontal axis tells you the wavelength. The vertical axis tells you what the instrument measured.

UV–Visible absorption

How much light did the sample take in?

The instrument sends light through the sample and compares the transmitted light with a reference. More absorption at a wavelength means higher absorbance there. The peak position is called λmax.

Fluorescence emission

How much light did the sample give off?

The fluorometer excites a sample at a chosen wavelength and measures the emitted light across many wavelengths. A higher peak means the detector recorded more light near that wavelength under the same settings.

Try it · Energy level diagram

Follow a molecule through three steps

A UV photon gives the molecule enough energy to jump from the ground state (S₀) to an excited state.

Why is the emitted light usually a longer wavelength?

Some of the energy is transferred to the surroundings before the molecule emits. Photon energy follows E = hc/λ. A lower energy corresponds to a longer wavelength.

The Stokes shift is the separation between the absorption and emission band maxima. The instrument’s selected excitation wavelength is a separate quantity.

What else can happen after excitation?

Vibrational relaxation and internal conversion

Vibrational relaxation moves between vibrational levels of the same electronic state. Internal conversion connects different electronic states of the same spin multiplicity. Both can transfer energy without light emission.

Fluorescence and phosphorescence

Fluorescence typically comes from an excited singlet state and is prompt. Phosphorescence comes from a triplet state and is typically slower. Only steady-state fluorescence spectra are discussed in this experiment.

A familiar example

Under a UV lamp, fluorescent highlighter ink can absorb UV and emit visible light. You see the emitted glow.

03 · Research gap and method

How do we make it a fair test?

Under this study’s conditions, does adding β-cyclodextrin produce a detectable change in salicylic acid’s light absorption or emission? Limited aqueous solubility motivated the work. The experiment tested for spectral changes.

Change

Host concentration

Prepare seven samples with β-cyclodextrin from 0 to 10 mM. The 0 mM sample still contains the fluorescent guest and serves as the comparison.

Keep fixed

Guest, pH and volume

10 µM salicylic acid in 10 mL at pH 7.0. pH matters because changing the molecule’s protonation can change its spectra.

Measure

Two light signals

Check absorption peak position and intensity. Examine fluorescence intensity across the emission spectrum at fixed excitation.

Try it · Sample preparation

Build one of the seven samples

Each flask receives 0.100 mL of 1 mM salicylic acid stock in methanol. Add host stock as needed, then bring the final volume to 10 mL.

Where does 10 µM come from?

The probe stock is diluted 100-fold. 1 mM × 0.100 mL ÷ 10.0 mL = 0.010 mM = 10 µM. Every sample contains the same amount of probe and carries the same nominal methanol fraction of 1% by volume.

What is the difference between a control and a blank?

The no-host control contains the probe, so it shows the probe’s response by itself. A blank omits the probe and helps account for background from the solvent or matrix. Quartz cuvettes transmit the UV wavelengths used here.

Observed

Emission rose modestly

The absorption band changed little. The emission peak was lowest in the no-host sample and highest at 10 mM host.

Interpretation

Inclusion is plausible

A less water-exposed, more restricted environment could reduce some energy-loss pathways. The trend is consistent with weak host–guest inclusion.

Follow-up

Test the explanation

Repeat independent samples and add error bars. An NMR experiment can look for close contacts between the guest and the host cavity.

Know the limits. The report describes spectral trends qualitatively. The plotted absorption series labels concentrations up to 8 mM, even though the preparation and emission series include 10 mM. Binding strength, exact geometry and improved solubility each require further evidence.

    Terms worth knowing

    Fluorophore
    A molecule or part of a molecule that can fluoresce.
    Absorbance
    A logarithmic measure of how much incident light is attenuated as it passes through a sample.
    Excitation wavelength
    The wavelength selected to illuminate the sample in the fluorescence measurement.
    Emission maximum
    The wavelength where a measured emission band has its highest intensity.
    Noncovalent interaction
    An association without forming a new covalent bond.
    Arbitrary units (a.u.)
    The instrument’s relative intensity scale. It supports comparison across samples measured under the same settings.
